## [OpenAI claims AI-generated solution to Navier–Stokes Millennium Prize Problem](https://openai.com/index/navier-stokes-solution) ⭐️ 10.0/10

OpenAI announced on September 8, 2026 that an unreleased internal frontier model, run as a swarm of roughly 10,000 AI agents, produced a counter-example showing breakdown of Navier–Stokes solutions in three-dimensional Euclidean space, together with a writeup and a formal proof in the Lean proof assistant. The agents reportedly reached the resolution on September 5, about 88 hours after launch, with Lean formalization and verification taking an additional 17 hours via GPT-6 Astra. If verified, this would be the first Millennium Prize Problem resolved with substantial AI involvement and only the second of the seven ever solved, marking a potential paradigm shift for both mathematics and AI-driven discovery. It also raises urgent questions about priority, attribution, and whether AI systems trained on researchers' private work can fairly claim credit for discoveries. The counter-example reportedly builds on a 2023 method by Diego Córdoba and Luis Martínez-Zoroa for finding blowup phenomena in related fluid equations, and OpenAI says it will not claim the $1 million Clay Millennium Prize; the result has not been verified by external mathematicians or the Clay Mathematics Institute. Across all attempted problems the agents sent 4.9 million messages and used about 300 billion output tokens, with roughly 130 billion output tokens and 2.7 million messages devoted to Navier–Stokes alone.

rss · OpenAI Blog · Sep 8, 10:00

**Background**: The Navier–Stokes existence and smoothness problem asks whether the equations describing fluid motion always have smooth, globally defined solutions in three-dimensional space and time, or whether solutions can 'blow up' in finite time; it was named one of seven Millennium Prize Problems by the Clay Mathematics Institute in 2000, each carrying a $1 million prize. Lean is a proof assistant and functional programming language that lets mathematicians write proofs as code so a computer can mechanically check every step, which is why a Lean formalization is treated as stronger evidence than an informal writeup. As of 2026 the only officially solved Millennium Prize Problem is the Poincaré conjecture, solved by Grigori Perelman, who declined the prize in 2010.

Tao frames good open problems as a non-renewable resource that is being mined unsustainably, and notes that even the mere rumor of someone working on a problem can trigger a massive AI-powered effort to solve it first. His latest comment extends that argument from resource depletion to a change in researcher incentives.

## [Google DeepMind Releases AlphaGenome Atlas of 9 Billion DNA Mutations](https://www.producthunt.com/products/alphagenome-atlas) ⭐️ 8.0/10

Google DeepMind announced AlphaGenome Atlas, a catalogue that predicts the molecular effects and AVI scores for all 9 billion possible single-nucleotide variants across the human genome. The Atlas was built by running the AlphaGenome AI model, released the previous year, across a reference human genome and comparing each reference base against every alternative. This is a major AI-driven advance in genomics: it gives researchers and clinicians a unified predictive map of how essentially any single-letter DNA change affects gene regulation, which could accelerate the interpretation of disease-linked variants and drug target discovery. It also shifts genomics from studying only the 2% coding regions to the 98% non-coding regions that orchestrate gene activity. AlphaGenome takes as input 1 Mb of DNA sequence, a much longer context than prior methods, which previously had to trade off input sequence length against prediction resolution. The Atlas covers single-nucleotide variants only, so it does not directly address insertions, deletions, or larger structural variants.

rss · Product Hunt (AI应用) · Sep 9, 01:41

**Background**: AlphaGenome is a unified DNA sequence model from Google DeepMind that predicts how genetic variants impact a wide range of biological processes regulating genes. Only about 2% of the human genome codes for proteins; the remaining 98%, called non-coding regions, is crucial for orchestrating gene activity and contains many variants linked to diseases. The Atlas extends this model by exhaustively scoring every possible single-letter change in the human genome.

## [NeurIPS desk-rejected 178 papers using a flawed AI detector](https://www.reddit.com/r/MachineLearning/comments/1wakf62/neurips_deskrejected_178_papers_for_being/) ⭐️ 8.0/10

NeurIPS's Position Paper Track used the proprietary Pangram AI detector to desk-reject 178 papers (18.4% of submissions) without human review or an appeal process. Independent researchers found the same detector flagged recent papers by the three track chairs at 24% to 69%, meaning the chairs would have failed their own test. This case exposes how unreliable black-box AI detectors are for high-stakes academic decisions, and it raises serious concerns about due process, fairness, and the treatment of non-native English speakers in peer review. If a top AI conference can reject nearly a fifth of submissions based on an uncalibrated proprietary score, other venues may follow suit with equally damaging results. The detector initially flagged 42.7% of the entire track, and Pangram's default setting marked nearly half of all submissions as 90-100% AI until the organizers shrank the text windows to bring the rate down to 12.7%. Twenty-two papers were rejected specifically because they scored above 0.5 while authors denied AI use, and a Stanford study cited in the post found 61.22% of human-written TOEFL essays are falsely flagged as AI, with NeurIPS publishing zero demographic calibration data.

reddit · r/MachineLearning · /u/tughanbulut · Sep 8, 10:19

**Background**: Pangram is an AI-text detection tool built by Brooklyn-based Pangram Labs that claims very high accuracy on fully AI-generated text, but independent studies have repeatedly shown such detectors produce many false positives and false negatives. The NeurIPS Position Paper Track is a newer, separate track from the main conference that solicits agenda-setting opinion pieces rather than original research, and its review criteria differ from the main track. Because AI detectors rely on statistical patterns rather than proof, using a single proprietary score as definitive evidence of misconduct is widely considered methodologically unsound.
