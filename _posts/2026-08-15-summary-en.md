## [Don't Classify, Hallucinate: A Novel Tagging Technique](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Aug/14/dont-classify-hallucinate/) ⭐️ 7.0/10

Doug Turnbull proposed a method to tag untagged content by having an LLM hallucinate candidate tags without a fixed vocabulary, then using vector embeddings to map them to the closest existing tags. Simon Willison highlighted this approach on his blog, noting its cleverness for handling his 1,856 tags. This technique offers a practical solution for content management systems with large tag vocabularies, where feeding all tags to an LLM is infeasible. It could streamline tagging workflows and inspire similar approaches in other domains, such as product categorization or document classification. The method involves prompting the LLM to generate novel tags based on the content, optionally providing examples of tag shapes to guide the model. Then, vector embeddings are used to find the closest existing tags to the hallucinated ones, effectively mapping them to the controlled vocabulary.

rss · Simon Willison · Aug 14, 21:54

**Background**: LLMs are known to hallucinate, producing plausible but incorrect information. However, this technique leverages hallucination as a feature rather than a bug. Vector embeddings represent text as numerical vectors, allowing semantic similarity to be measured, which is key to mapping hallucinated tags to existing ones.
