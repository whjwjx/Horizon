## [Delhi Slashes Electricity Losses from 50% to 5%](https://spectrum.ieee.org/delhi-electricity-loss) ⭐️ 8.0/10

Delhi has dramatically reduced its electricity losses from roughly 50 percent to about 5 percent, according to an IEEE Spectrum case study. The achievement came through a combination of technical upgrades and policy measures targeting both technical inefficiencies and rampant electricity theft. This case study shows that even severe distribution losses in developing-world megacities can be reversed, offering a replicable model for other Indian states and emerging economies. It also matters because reducing losses improves utility finances, cuts emissions, and eliminates chronic load shedding that once disrupted daily life. The losses were not purely technical: theft was rampant among businesses, residents, and even utility employees who siphoned power from streetlights and nearby distribution lines. Insulating power lines to prevent theft had an unintended side effect of giving monkeys safe 'roads' between neighborhoods and access to upper floors of apartment buildings.

hackernews · rbanffy · Sep 29, 12:43 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49892245)

**Background**: Aggregate Technical and Commercial (AT&C) losses measure the gap between electricity supplied to a distribution network and electricity actually billed and collected, combining technical losses (heat, resistance) with commercial losses (theft, non-payment). In many developing countries these losses exceed 20-30 percent, crippling utility finances. India has pursued smart grid modernization through initiatives like the India Smart Grid Forum to improve metering, monitoring, and theft detection.

## [Anthropic: GLM-5.3 and Claude Mythos Cross Binary Exploitation Threshold](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Sep/29/anthropic-frontier-red-team/) ⭐️ 8.0/10

Anthropic's Frontier Red Team evaluated several models on 100 randomly selected tasks from its internal Binary Exploitation benchmark and found that GLM-5.3 achieved full control flow hijacks in 4% of trials, while Claude Mythos Preview did so in 6%. Earlier models such as Claude Opus 4.6 and GLM-5.2 succeeded in none of the trials. This marks the first time these models have crossed a threshold that earlier systems could not, signaling that advanced offensive cyber capabilities are spreading beyond a single lab's frontier models. It has direct implications for AI security research, vulnerability disclosure policy, and how open-weight models like GLM-5.3 are governed. The evaluation used 100 randomly selected tasks from an internal Binary Exploitation benchmark, and the reported figures are success rates for full control flow hijacks rather than partial progress. GLM-5.3 is Z.ai's open-weights flagship model, released on 14 August 2026 with weights published two weeks later, while Claude Mythos Preview was not publicly released due to its vulnerability-finding abilities.

rss · Simon Willison · Sep 29, 22:20

**Background**: A control flow hijack is a classic memory-corruption attack in which an adversary redirects a program's execution, for example through a buffer overflow or return-oriented programming, to run code of their choosing. Binary exploitation is the practice of finding and weaponizing such flaws in compiled software without access to source code. Anthropic's Frontier Red Team studies the offensive cyber capabilities of frontier AI models, and this result comes from its research on GLM-5.3 and the spread of advanced cyber capabilities.

This incident highlights how vulnerable the npm supply chain is, since a single compromised package can silently spread malware to countless downstream projects and steal developer credentials. The worm propagates by scanning for SSH access and re-uploading itself to remote hosts, similar to previously documented npm worms like Shai-Hulud that infected over 180 packages and exfiltrated secrets to GitHub.

Because npm historically requires minimal authentication and no mandatory code review for updates, attackers can publish malicious versions of popular packages that execute during installation. Self-replicating worms are especially dangerous because they use stolen credentials to infect additional packages, creating a cascading supply chain compromise.
